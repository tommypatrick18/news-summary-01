President Donald Trump signed a bill Wednesday night to end the record 43-day US government shutdown after a 222-209 House vote; the AP reported the legislation funds operations through January 30 but does not extend ACA health subsidies. In Abu Dhabi, Russia and Ukraine concluded two days of US-brokered talks by agreeing to a major swap of up to 1,500 prisoners each, though Reuters noted trust remains low as mediators Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow on Thursday with territory unresolved. Security alarms rose as NATO jets scrambled when at least four drones entered Baltic airspace during strikes on Russia; Reuters and broadcaster LRT reported that Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia issued alerts, and Lithuania temporarily closed Vilnius and Kaunas airports. At COP31 in Antalya, Reuters said UN chief Antonio Guterres warned 102 leaders of a "path to mutually assured destruction," while Brazil and Turkey said wars must not distract from climate action. Per Reuters and Xinhua, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi held a "positive" call with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio ahead of an expected Xi-Trump meeting in Shenzhen. Iran's Foreign Ministry designated Argentina’s navy and air force as terrorist organizations in a reciprocal move reported by Reuters. Gaza storms flooded camps for over 940,000 displaced people, with 150,000 tents needed, per the Guardian and BBC. Government data from the Democratic Republic of Congo confirmed the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak killed 5,387 out of 11,042 cases, its deadliest ever. Reuters reported a Chinese university with military ties purchased two blacklisted Nvidia H200 AI chips for $123,000 despite US bans. The FT said Anthropic bankers may raise its IPO price above $78, valuing it at $1.5 trillion. Reuters reported hot 0.4% US inflation made a December Fed hike nearly certain, while Fitch placed Paraguay’s BB+ rating on watch after the collapse of lender ueno froze the savings of nearly 3 million customers.
